Lettuce Growth Model — Formula & Constant Reference

The simulator runs two independent growth models for lettuce side by side: a phenomenological Traditional (Gompertz / Liebig) model shared across all five species, and a mechanistic Van Henten (1994) two-state ODE model specific to lettuce. This page documents every formula and constant each one uses, exactly as implemented in backend/simulator.py.

Model A · Phenomenological

Traditional (Gompertz / Liebig) Model

A sigmoidal maturity curve driven by accumulated Growing Degree Days (GDD), with five independent stress factors (light, temperature, nutrients, pH, water/EC) throttling both the pace of development and the final quality grade. Used for all five species; values below are lettuce-specific.

Formulas

  1. Gompertz maturity curve
    $$ M(x) = \exp\!\big(-B \cdot e^{-k x}\big) $$
    Universal sigmoid: slow establishment → fast bulking → plateau near harvest.
  2. Temperature quality factor
    $$ f_T(T)=\begin{cases} 0 & T\le T_{base}\ \text{or}\ T\ge T_{max}\\[2pt] \dfrac{T-T_{base}}{T_{optLow}-T_{base}} & T_{base}<T<T_{optLow}\\[6pt] 1 & T_{optLow}\le T\le T_{optHigh}\\[4pt] \max\!\Big(0,\,1-\dfrac{T-T_{optHigh}}{T_{max}-T_{optHigh}}\Big) & T>T_{optHigh} \end{cases} $$
    Floor / plateau / ceiling productivity response — photosynthetic efficiency, not raw phenology.
  3. Daily GDD accumulation
    $$ \text{gdd}(T) = \begin{cases} T-T_{base} & T_{base}<T<T_{max}\\ 0 & \text{otherwise}\end{cases} $$
    Degrees above base, zeroed outside the viable window — paces the phenological clock.
  4. Daily Light Integral
    $$ \text{DLI} = \text{PPFD} \times \text{lightHours} \times 0.0036 $$
    Converts instantaneous PPFD (µmol·m⁻²·s⁻¹) and photoperiod into mol·m⁻²·day⁻¹.
  5. DLI factor (saturating)
    $$ f_{DLI} = 1-e^{-\text{DLI}/h}, \qquad h = \dfrac{\text{DLI}_{optimal}}{2.3} $$
    Diminishing-returns response to total daily light; reaches ~90% right at the species optimum.
  6. Photoinhibition factor
    $$ \text{slope}=\dfrac{P_{max}}{\text{PPFD}_{ceiling}-\text{PPFD}_{thresh}},\qquad f_{PI}=1-\min\!\big(P_{max},\ \max(0,\text{PPFD}-\text{PPFD}_{thresh})\cdot\text{slope}\big) $$
    Separate mechanism from DLI saturation: penalizes sustained excess instantaneous PPFD regardless of duration.
  7. Combined light factor
    $$ f_{light} = f_{DLI}\times f_{PI} $$
  8. pH factor
    $$ f_{pH} = \max\!\Big(0,\ 1-\dfrac{|\text{pH}-\text{pH}_{optimal}|}{\text{pH}_{tolerance}}\Big) $$
  9. Per-nutrient score
    $$ s_i = \max\!\Big(0,\ 1-\dfrac{|c_i-\text{opt}_i|}{\text{opt}_i}\Big),\quad i\in\{N,P,K,Ca,Mg\} $$
  10. Nutrient sufficiency — Liebig's Law of the Minimum
    $$ f_{nut} = \min_i\, s_i $$
    Growth is bottlenecked by whichever single nutrient is scarcest relative to its optimum (Liebig, 1840s).
  11. Solution EC
    $$ EC = \dfrac{\sum_i c_i}{700},\qquad EC_{optimal}=\dfrac{\sum_i \text{opt}_i}{700} $$
  12. EC factor (osmotic ceiling)
    $$ f_{EC}=\begin{cases} 1 & EC\le EC_{optimal}\cdot 1.3\\[4pt] \max\!\Big(0.15,\ 1-\dfrac{EC-EC_{optimal}\cdot1.3}{EC_{optimal}\cdot(2.0-1.3)}\Big) & \text{otherwise} \end{cases} $$
    Distinct from Liebig's law: penalizes total salt load even when every individual nutrient is sufficient.
  13. Non-thermal throttle & growth rate
    $$ \eta = f_{light}\cdot f_{nut}\cdot f_{pH}\cdot f_{water}\cdot f_{EC},\qquad G=\eta\cdot f_T $$
    A starved-of-light plant develops slower even if warm — thermal pacing and condition quality are modeled separately.
  14. Stress index
    $$ S = 1-\min(f_{nut},f_{pH},f_{water},f_{EC},f_T) $$
  15. Pseudo-age & maturity
    $$ x = \dfrac{\sum \text{gdd}(T)\cdot\eta}{GDD_{target}}\times REF,\qquad \mu = \min\!\Big(1,\ \dfrac{M(x)}{NORM}\Big) $$
  16. Leaf count, biomass, canopy
    $$ L=L_0+(L_h-L_0)\mu,\qquad W=\mu^{1.5}\cdot W_h,\qquad C=3+(C_h-3)\mu $$
  17. Harvest & grading
    $$ \text{harvested when } \mu\ge 0.95 $$
    Grade from stress S: A (<0.10) · B (<0.30) · C (<0.60) · D (≥0.60) · F (never reaches harvest).
Model B · Mechanistic ODE

Van Henten (1994) Model

A two-state dynamic model integrating structural (Xsdm) and non-structural (Xnsdm) dry matter via coupled ODEs, with photosynthesis co-limited by light and CO₂ through a Farquhar-style term. Lettuce-only; runs alongside Model A rather than replacing it. Source: Van Henten, E.J. (1994), Agricultural Systems 45(1), 55–72; cross-checked against Van Ooteghem (2007).

Formulas

  1. CO₂ compensation point
    $$ \Gamma(T) = \Gamma_{20}\cdot Q10_{\Gamma}^{\,(T-20)/10} $$
  2. pH-gated nutrient availability (per lever)
    $$ a(\text{pH}) = \begin{cases} 0 & \text{pH}\le lo_{min}\ \text{or}\ \text{pH}\ge hi_{max}\\[3pt] \dfrac{\text{pH}-lo_{min}}{lo-lo_{min}} & lo_{min}<\text{pH}<lo\\[6pt] 1 & lo\le\text{pH}\le hi\\[4pt] \dfrac{hi_{max}-\text{pH}}{hi_{max}-hi} & hi<\text{pH}<hi_{max} \end{cases} $$
  3. Nutrient photosynthetic capacity
    $$ \text{cap} = 1-\exp\!\Big(\!-\dfrac{\text{ppm}\cdot a(\text{pH})}{\text{opt}/2.3}\Big) $$
    Computed independently for N (Rubisco), K (stomata), Mg (chlorophyll) — reaches ~90% at species optimum.
  4. Combined stomatal-conductance multiplier
    $$ g_{mult} = \text{cap}_K \cdot f_{water}\cdot f_{EC} $$
    K, water stress and EC all gate the same guard-cell turgor mechanism, so they combine multiplicatively (Jarvis, 1976). f_EC reuses the Model A EC-factor formula.
  5. Quantum (light-use) efficiency
    $$ \varepsilon = \varepsilon_0\cdot\text{cap}_{Mg}\cdot\dfrac{CO_2-\Gamma}{CO_2+2\Gamma} $$
  6. Irradiance & light-limited term
    $$ I=\dfrac{\text{PPFD}}{4.6},\qquad L_{term}=\varepsilon\cdot I $$
  7. Carboxylation conductance
    $$ g_{car}(T)=\max\!\big(10^{-6},\ -1.32\!\times\!10^{-5}T^2+5.94\!\times\!10^{-4}T-2.64\!\times\!10^{-3}\big) $$
  8. Effective stomatal & canopy CO₂ conductance
    $$ g_{stm,eff}=\max(10^{-6},\ g_{stm}\cdot g_{mult}),\qquad g_{CO_2}=\dfrac{1}{\tfrac{1}{g_{bnd}}+\tfrac{1}{g_{stm,eff}}+\tfrac{1}{g_{car}}} $$
    Boundary-layer, stomatal and carboxylation resistances in series.
  9. CO₂-limited term
    $$ C_{term} = g_{CO_2}\cdot\omega\cdot(CO_2-\Gamma)\cdot\text{cap}_N $$
  10. Farquhar-style co-limitation
    $$ f_{phot,max}^{raw} = \dfrac{L_{term}\cdot C_{term}}{L_{term}+C_{term}} $$
  11. Photoinhibition penalty
    $$ f_{phot,max} = f_{phot,max}^{raw}\cdot f_{PI}(\text{PPFD}) $$
    The 1994 model has no photoinhibition term of its own — this reuses Model A's f_PI so both paths agree at high PPFD instead of Van Henten extrapolating unbounded growth past the plant's real tolerance.
  12. Canopy light interception
    $$ CC = 1-\exp\!\big(-K\cdot LAR\cdot(1-\tau)\cdot X_{sdm}\big) $$
  13. Gross canopy photosynthesis
    $$ f_{phot} = CC\cdot f_{phot,max} $$
  14. Maintenance respiration
    $$ q10_{resp}=Q10_{resp}^{(T-25)/10},\qquad f_{resp}=\big(c_{sht}(1\!-\!\tau)X_{sdm}+c_{rt}\,\tau X_{sdm}\big)\cdot q10_{resp} $$
  15. Specific growth rate
    $$ r_{gr} = c_{gr,max}\cdot\dfrac{X_{nsdm}}{\gamma X_{sdm}+X_{nsdm}}\cdot Q10_{gr}^{(T-20)/10} $$
    Structural growth throttled by how full the non-structural sugar buffer currently is.
  16. Structural dry-matter ODE
    $$ \dfrac{dX_{sdm}}{dt} = r_{gr}\cdot X_{sdm} $$
  17. Growth (synthesis) respiration
    $$ R_{growth} = \dfrac{1-\beta}{\beta}\cdot r_{gr}\cdot X_{sdm} $$
  18. Non-structural buffer ODE
    $$ \dfrac{dX_{nsdm}}{dt} = \alpha\cdot f_{phot} - r_{gr}X_{sdm} - f_{resp} - R_{growth} $$
    At night (PPFD = 0), f_phot = 0 — the buffer drains while structural growth keeps drawing on it.
  19. Numerical integration
    $$ X(t+\Delta t) = \max(0,\ X(t) + \tfrac{dX}{dt}\Delta t) $$
    Explicit Euler, 24 substeps across each day's lit phase (light hours) and dark phase (24 − light hours) separately — not a flat 24 h of light.
  20. Biomass partition & yield conversion
    $$ \text{leaf}=(1\!-\!\tau)(X_{sdm}\!+\!X_{nsdm}),\quad \text{root}=\tau(X_{sdm}\!+\!X_{nsdm}) $$
    $$ \text{total}_{plant}=\dfrac{X_{sdm}+X_{nsdm}}{\text{density}},\qquad \text{fresh}_{plant}=\dfrac{\text{total}_{plant}}{0.05} $$
    Harvest day = first day fresh_plant ≥ harvestTargetG.
Shared machinery. Both models run for every lettuce simulation and are reported side by side. They share three building blocks by design rather than duplicating them: the EC/osmotic-ceiling formula (§EC factor), the photoinhibition formula (§Photoinhibition factor), and the lettuce species constants table (temperatures, pH, nutrient optima, EC multipliers). Nitrogen, potassium and magnesium each drive a distinct physiological lever inside Van Henten (Rubisco / stomata / chlorophyll respectively); phosphorus and calcium have no equivalent single-parameter hook in the 1994 model's two-term co-limitation and therefore only affect the Traditional model.
